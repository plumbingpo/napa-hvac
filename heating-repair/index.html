When the heat goes out in Napa, CA, the clock starts ticking, and the diagnosis depends entirely on what kind of system is doing the failing. On a gas furnace, the most common no-heat call we run is a flame sensor coated in oxide: the burners light, the sensor cannot prove the flame, and the board shuts the gas down three tries later and locks out. Close behind come cracked hot surface igniters, stuck pressure switches, clogged condensate traps on high-efficiency models, and blower motors that have quietly worn out. Heat pumps fail differently: a weak defrost cycle leaves the outdoor coil buried under frost, a failed reversing valve strands the system in cooling, and a dead outdoor unit leaves the electric backup strips carrying the whole house at several times the operating cost.

Fault codes are where our diagnosis starts, not where it ends. On every no-heat call, Napa HVAC confirms the board's story with measurements: flame signal read in microamps, igniter resistance checked, the pressure switch tested against real inducer draft, capacitors and motors metered, and, on heat pumps, refrigerant charge and defrost operation verified. Any visit that touches the gas side also covers the safety basics. We leak-test the connections, look over the heat exchanger, and confirm the flue is drafting, since no heating repair is finished until it has been proven safe.

Why Professional Heating Repair Matters

Heating repair carries more risk than any other job in home comfort work because combustion is involved. A furnace that lights late booms gas vapor inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal; a heat exchanger that cracks can pass combustion gases into the supply air; a flue that has stopped drafting sends carbon monoxide into the house instead of out of it, and none of these announce themselves in ways an untrained eye can catch. The diagnostic terrain is genuinely tricky too: a furnace that short-cycles might have a failing limit switch, or it might be protecting itself from a clogged filter, and cleaning a flame sensor without asking why it fouled leaves the next lockout already scheduled.

That is why Napa HVAC does not work on hunches. Our technicians read flame signal on a meter, look at the heat exchanger directly, confirm the flue is drafting, and compare temperature rise to the data plate so the blower and the fire are properly matched. On heat pumps we check defrost controls, sensors, and the reversing valve rather than blaming the compressor, and we find out whether the electric backup heat has been running unnoticed and driving up your costs. Nothing is called fixed until it has run a full cycle and proven itself safe and correct, because your family's warmth and safety depend on it.

Heating Repair Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly and put one on every level of the home
  • Keep chlorine products, paints, and solvents away from the furnace, because their fumes corrode burners
  • Replace filters monthly through the heating season to keep airflow strong and the heat exchanger from overheating
  • Leave open space around the furnace so the burners can draw enough combustion air
  • Book a fall tune-up so worn parts are caught before the first cold night
  • Do not ignore a boom at ignition or a new rattle, since early repairs cost far less
  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
